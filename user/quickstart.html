Playcraft Suite quick start#

Playground design tools for AutoCAD 2027. You place real catalog parts, the posts and fillers are added for you, and the parts list and price update as you work.

The six commands you will use every day:

Command What it does
PBCATALOG Opens the catalog. Find a part and double-click it to place it.
PBSTAMP Places parts one after another, snapped to the parts already there.
PBGEN Checks the posts and puts them right.
PBFILL Places barrier fillers between decks of different heights.
PBPANEL Opens a live parts list with prices.
PBBOM Prints the parts list, one section per structure.

PBHELLO prints the build you have loaded. Put that line in anything you report.

Install#

  1. Open https://github.com/playcraftSystems/PlaycraftSuite/releases/latest/download/Playcraft.bundle.zip in a browser signed in to a GitHub account that has access. Without access you get a 404.
  2. Extract the whole zip. The installer does not run from inside the zip.
  3. Run install.cmd. It installs for your user only and needs no admin rights.
  4. Restart AutoCAD 2027.

You need Windows x64, AutoCAD 2027 and access to the component library folder. A pricing token is optional. Without one, prices come from the built-in price list.

  • To update: run the new zip’s install.cmd.
  • To uninstall: delete %APPDATA%\Autodesk\ApplicationPlugins\Playcraft.bundle.
  • To check it loaded: type PBHELLO. If AutoCAD says Unknown command, the zip was not fully extracted before install.cmd ran.

First run#

The setup window opens by itself the first time. PBSETUP opens it again.

  1. Browse to the folder that holds the family folders (Access Components, Decks, and so on). The window shows what it found, such as 233 families, 6516 components. 0 families means you picked the wrong folder.
  2. Paste a pricing token if you have one and press Test connection. You can leave it blank.

Without a library, parts place as labeled placeholder blocks.

Your first layout#

  1. Type PBCATALOG.
  2. Type square deck and double-click the first row.
  3. Press Enter to accept height 48.
  4. Click at 0,0, then at 48,0, then press Enter.

You now have two decks side by side with their posts. Each part brings its own posts. Where two parts share a post there is one post, sized for the taller part.

  1. Type PBPANEL. The panel counts and prices the decks and posts, and updates after every edit.

One UNDO takes back a part and its posts together.

Place parts#

PBSTAMP#

PBSTAMP keeps placing the part you chose. The part follows the cursor and snaps to matching connection points. The words at the cursor say what it is snapped to, or why it cannot attach and where it could. Click inside a placed deck to stack the next storey on it. Click to place, and press Enter to finish.

While placing, type:

Type What it does
Next Attaches by the other side. The words say which side, such as “2 of 2”.
Angle Asks for an angle, such as 45 or -30, for parts that do not snap.
Rotate / Counter Turns the part 15° one way or the other.
Turn Turns the part 180°.
Free Turns the snapping off, and on again.

The angle stays for the rest of the run, so a row of benches all face the same way. A part that snaps takes its angle from what it attaches to.

Over a post, a panel swings around that post with the cursor. Aim it and click, and its far post is placed with it.

Turn parts already placed#

Select them and type PBROT15 (counter-clockwise) or PBROTBACK15 (clockwise). Each turns 15°. The ⟲ ⟳ buttons on the catalog do the same. A selection that includes a deck turns as one structure around its first deck.

To line a part up with a fence, use AutoCAD’s ROTATE with the Reference option: pick two points along the part, type Points, then pick two points along the fence.

Place by hand#

Plain AutoCAD works too. A part you move into place by hand gets the same posts, fillers and price as one that snapped.

  • Every connection point, posts included, is a Node snap target. MOVE a part by its connection point onto its neighbour’s.
  • After a MOVE, COPY, ROTATE, STRETCH or grip edit, the command line says what followed, such as Posts follow the MOVE: 2 added, 0 re-sized, 4 removed.
  • A part left a little short is not attached. The command line says so, such as S-1101-R5 at (0, 0) is 2" from S-1101-R5 at (43, 0): not attached. MOVE it again with Node snap. PBAUDIT lists every such pair.
  • A block put in with AutoCAD’s INSERT counts after its next edit.
  • One UNDO takes back a move and everything that followed it.

Roofs#

Roofs, shade canopies and toppers go on last.

  1. Set the structure’s age range first (the settings row, or PBSTRUCTURE).
  2. PBSTAMP the roof and snap it to any edge of the deck. It lands over the deck’s centre.
  3. The command line says the height it took, such as Roof over 36" deck → 105" (69" clearance, ages 2-5).
  • The height is the highest deck under the roof’s posts, rounded up to whole feet, plus the roof’s clearance for the age range. The roof’s posts are sized for it.
  • A roof takes no deck edge. Parts already on the edges do not stop it.
  • With no age range set, or the snapping off, the command asks for the deck height under the roof. Type a height, or Ground for a roof with no deck.
  • PBHEIGHT on a deck moves the roof over it, and keeps a raise.
  • To raise a roof, type PBHEIGHT, then Roof, click the roof and type the height. Roofs go up in 12" steps from their lowest height.
  • The roof’s height label shows on plan. Editing it by hand is checked: the command line says when a roof is below its lowest height, and PBAUDIT lists it.
  • PBSETTINGS roofoverride=1 adds 1 ft (0 to 3) to every roof and shade canopy placed after it. The design file’s Roof Height Override is read on import and written on export.

Set up each structure#

The settings row at the top of PBCATALOG says what you are building next.

Setting Choices
Age 2-5, 5-12, 2-12, 6-23, 9-14, 13+ or 14+
System R5, R35, RH5, RH3 or RV5
Mounting In ground or Surface mount
Posts Steel or Aluminum
Deck A deck height. PBSTAMP offers it first when you place a deck.
Show Structure parts, Independent items (swings, freestanding, fitness and site items) or All
IPEMA only Tick box
Canada Tick box

Each click filters the catalog and the command line says what was set, such as Drawing default set: ages 2-5, R5. One UNDO takes back one click. If a command is still running, the click waits: press Enter or Esc and click again.

What the row does#

  • The catalog hides parts that do not fit. The line under the row says how many, such as 38 parts hidden by the drawing's settings (ages 2-5, R5). Tick Show hidden parts to see them, each with its reason. You can still place a hidden part. The command line tells you it is hidden and why.
  • Parts you place take the row’s settings. Each part keeps the settings it was placed with. Changing the row does not change parts already placed, or their posts.
  • Deck heights are checked for the age. PBSTAMP and PBHEIGHT refuse a height the deck is not rated for, such as S-1101-R5 is not rated for 84" in a 2-5 structure (its 2-5 band rates 6"-72").
  • Posts follow mounting and material. Surface mount and aluminum change the post part numbers, such as S-1008-R35-08ft-SMA.
  • A deck’s posts match the deck. An R5 deck gets R5 posts and an R35 deck gets R35 posts, whatever the row says.

Build the next structure#

Change the row and keep placing. The parts you already placed keep their settings.

Change parts already placed#

  1. Select the parts.
  2. Click Apply the row to selection under the row.

The parts take the row’s settings and their posts are re-picked. Parts that do not fit are listed with the reason and selected. One UNDO takes it back.

When you select parts placed with different settings from the row, a line under the row says so, such as 12 selected parts were placed with ages 2-5, R35.

  • Use these puts those settings in the row.
  • Apply the row to them gives the parts the row’s settings.

Copies#

COPY, MIRROR and ARRAY copies keep the settings of the parts they were copied from.

Canada#

Tick Canada in the row. The catalog hides parts made only for the US, and placed parts made only for the US are listed. Canada is one setting for the whole drawing.

Clear all#

Clear all sets every setting in the row back to not set, turns Canada off, sets Show to All, and clears the search, family, price, fit filter and sort. Placed parts keep their settings.

Imported designs and named structures#

  • Importing a .kcd fills the row from the tab you import.
  • PBSTRUCTURE gives a named group settings of its own. Click any part of the group and answer the prompts. Press Enter to keep a value.
  • When you select a group with its own settings, a line under the row shows them, with Use these and Save the row to the group.

From the keyboard#

PBSETTINGS does what a click does. Type one setting at a time:

Type What it does
age=2-5, system=R5, mounting=surface, material=aluminum, ipema=yes, region=Canada, deck=48, show=structure Sets one setting in the row. An empty value, such as age=, sets it back to not set.
apply Gives the selected parts the row’s settings.
use Puts the selected parts’ settings in the row.
use=TOWER Puts the TOWER group’s settings in the row.
saveto=TOWER Gives the TOWER group the row’s settings.
clear Sets the whole row back to not set.

Name each structure for the quote#

Group each play structure and give it a name. The parts list and the quote have one section per named group.

Parts in no group are listed by family, such as Decks, Posts and Freestanding Slides. Site items such as benches and trash cans go under Site Amenities. Swings, freestanding items and site amenities can stay loose. Group a structure to list it under its own name.

To name a structure:

  1. Type CLASSICGROUP.
  2. Type the name under Group Name and click New.
  3. Select the structure’s parts and press Enter.
  4. Click OK.

The name you type is the heading on the quote, such as 5-12 Composite. AutoCAD does not allow / in a name, so write 5-12, not 5/12.

  • Posts, fillers and parts that snap onto a grouped part join its group.
  • A swing set saved as its own drawing and brought in with XREF gets its own section.
  • Do not make a structure into a block. Parts inside a block are left out of the parts list and the quote.

Start from a design you already have#

  • A .kcd design file: PBKCDINFO shows what is in each tab without changing the drawing. PBIMPORTKCD brings one tab in at its exact positions and heights. Importing the same tab again replaces it.
  • An older flat Playcraft drawing: PBMIGRATEZ lifts parts to their real heights. Parts it cannot place for certain stay where they are and are listed. You can run it more than once.

PBEXPORTKCD writes the drawing back out to a .kcd. PBDEPS checks a .kcd for missing attachments and broken links.

An imported design is fully built, so most connection points are already taken. If a part will not snap, run PBMATES: grey points are taken.

Find a part in the catalog#

PBCATALOG opens with the cursor in the search box. The best matches come first.

Type Finds
square deck Parts with both words, in any order.
"sit down" Parts with that exact phrase.
slide -castle Slides, leaving out parts with “castle”.
S-1101-R5, s1101r5 That part. Dashes, spaces and case do not matter.
1101 That part in every rail system.
castel Close misspellings still match. The panel shows what it searched for.

The family and price filters and the settings row work together with the search. Click a column header to sort, and click it again to reverse. Parts with no price show a dash and stay at the bottom.

Storeys and the rulebook#

PBLEVEL#

PBLEVEL lists which storey each part stands on. Type a level number to select everything on it. Answer Layers to put each storey on its own layer (PB_LVL0, PB_LVL1, and so on). Freeze a layer to hide that storey while you work on the one above. Frozen storeys are still counted and exported. Delete the PB_LVL layers to stop.

PBAUDIT#

PBAUDIT checks the drawing against the design team’s rules and lists what breaks them:

  • decks 108" and up with an open edge;
  • stepping distances, Geo-Wave and traverse heights;
  • ground panels under a deck too tall for them;
  • the wrong limb climber for the deck;
  • mirrored or scaled parts;
  • parts that almost touch;
  • parts that do not fit the settings they were placed with.

Each finding names the part and the wiki card the rule comes from. PBAUDIT only reports. It never changes the drawing.

Plain AutoCAD#

MOVE, COPY, ERASE, ROTATE, layers, groups, xrefs, Properties and UNDO all work on Playcraft parts. Posts follow MOVE, COPY, ERASE and grips. PBGEN checks them. PBFILL brings the fillers up to date.

Watch out for these:

Command What happens
EXPLODE The part becomes plain lines and leaves the quote. UNDO brings it back. Change its layer or colour instead.
MOVE with an object snap The part takes the height of the point you snapped to. The command line says so. Use PBHEIGHT to change heights. OSNAPZ set to 1 stops snaps changing heights.
COPY of an imported part The copy shares the original’s identity until you run PBEXPORTKCD, which gives each copy its own.
MIRROR, SCALE The part number does not change, so it is priced as the standard part. Handed parts have their own part numbers in the catalog.

Commands also list what they skipped, such as parts on frozen layers or inside blocks. These lines are not errors.

New in this build#

  • Roofs take their height from the deck and the age range. Snap a roof to a deck edge and its height and posts are worked out. See Roofs.
  • Loose parts are listed by family. Decks, posts and fillers in no group go under their family, such as Decks and Posts, not Unassigned.
  • Plain messages. What the plugin prints on the command line and in the panel is short and plain, like this guide.
  • Parts keep the settings they were placed with. Change the row to start the next structure. Parts already placed, and their posts, do not change.
  • A deck’s posts match the deck’s own system.
  • Apply the row to selection gives selected parts the row’s settings.
  • Copies keep their settings.

See Set up each structure.

Every command#

* means the command needs the component library.

Setup#

Command What it does
PBSETUP Opens the setup window for the library folder and pricing token.
PBVAULT Sets the library folder from the command line.

Place parts#

Command What it does
PBCATALOG * Opens the catalog. Search, filter, sort, and double-click a part to place it.
PBFIT * Click a placed part. The catalog then lists only parts that fit it.
PBSTAMP * Places parts one after another, snapped to the parts already there.
PBROT15, PBROTBACK15 Turns the selected parts 15° counter-clockwise or clockwise.
PBATTACH * Click near a deck edge to attach the 24" Poly Wave Slide.
PBMATES Shows every connection point: green is free, grey is taken. Run it again to hide them.

Structure#

Command What it does
PBGEN Checks every post and puts it right.
PBFILL * Places barrier fillers where decks of different heights share posts.
PBHEIGHT Changes a deck’s height. Click a deck, or press Enter for every deck.
PBSETTINGS Sets the catalog’s settings row from the keyboard.
PBSTRUCTURE Gives a named group settings of its own.
PBREGION Sets the drawing to US or Canada.
PBLEVEL Lists storeys, selects one, or puts each on its own layer.
PBMIGRATEZ Lifts an older flat drawing to real heights.

3D#

Command What it does
PB3D * Opens a live 3D view. Drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Click a part to select it.

Count and price#

Command What it does
PBBOM Prints the parts list, one section per structure. Works offline.
PBPRICE Prints the parts list with MSRP and REP prices.
PBEXPORTBOM Saves the priced parts list as a CSV for Excel.
PBPANEL Opens a live parts list with prices.
PBSYNC Refreshes the catalog data saved in the drawing. Needs the pricing token.

Design files (.kcd)#

Command What it does
PBKCDINFO Shows the part, post and filler counts in each tab of a .kcd.
PBIMPORTKCD * Imports one tab of a .kcd.
PBEXPORTKCD * Writes the drawing to a .kcd, plus a .json copy.
PBDEPS * Checks a .kcd for missing attachments and broken links.

Checks#

Command What it does
PBAUDIT Checks the drawing against the design team’s rules.
PBHELLO Prints the build you have loaded.
PBNUDGE Used by test scripts. Use MOVE instead.
PBLIBCHECK Checks the part library. For library maintainers.

If something looks wrong#

Problem What to do
Parts place as placeholder blocks The library is not found. Run PBSETUP.
A part will not snap Run PBMATES. Grey means the point is taken. If it is green and still will not snap, report it.
A part is missing from the catalog The settings row hides it. Tick Show hidden parts.
A search finds nothing Check the settings row and family filter, or click Clear all. A leading - leaves a word out, so remove it from a pasted part number.
A part is missing from the quote It was exploded. UNDO brings it back.
PBBOM lists a structure’s decks under Decks, not its name Its parts are in no named group. See Name each structure for the quote.
One structure shows as two sections Its parts are in two groups. Put them in one.
A whole structure is missing from the parts list It was made into a block. Explode the block and group the parts.
Two parts look joined but get extra posts They are a little apart. MOVE one with Node snap.
Setup says 0 families Pick the folder that holds the family folders.
PB commands are missing Check you have AutoCAD 2027 and that the zip was fully extracted before install.cmd ran.
No live prices Run PBSETUP, paste the token and press Test connection. Built-in prices still work.

More help: INSTALL.md and the User Guide.

Tell us what you find#

Send four lines:

Build:      (the line PBHELLO prints)
Doing:      what you did, in order
Expected:   what you thought would happen
Happened:   what actually happened

The Expected line tells us whether it is a bug or a different idea of how it should work.

Things worth telling us:

  • where a part would not snap and the words at the cursor did not say why;
  • a count, price or post height that is wrong;
  • a part you searched for that was not in the first few rows (tell us what you typed);
  • a PBAUDIT finding you would have signed off, or a problem it missed.

A screenshot of the command line helps. The drawing helps most.
