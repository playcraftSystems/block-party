Playcraft Suite quick start#

Playground design tools for AutoCAD 2027. You place real catalog parts, the posts and fillers are added for you, and the parts list and price update as you work.

The six commands you will use every day:

Command What it does
PBCATALOG Opens the catalog. Find a part and double-click it to place it.
PBSTAMP Places parts one after another, snapped to the parts already there.
PBGEN Checks the posts and puts them right.
PBFILL Places barrier fillers between decks of different heights.
PBPANEL Opens a live parts list with prices, one section per structure.
PBBOM Prints the parts list, one section per structure.

PBHELLO prints the build you have loaded. Put that line in anything you report.

Install#

  1. Open https://github.com/playcraftSystems/PlaycraftSuite/releases/latest/download/Playcraft.bundle.zip in a browser signed in to a GitHub account that has access. Without access you get a 404.
  2. Extract the whole zip. The installer does not run from inside the zip.
  3. Run install.cmd. It installs for your user only and needs no admin rights.
  4. Restart AutoCAD 2027.

You need Windows x64, AutoCAD 2027 and access to the component library folder. A pricing token is optional. Without one, prices come from the built-in price list.

  • To update: run the new zip’s install.cmd.
  • To uninstall: close AutoCAD and run uninstall.cmd from the zip. It asks whether to keep your saved settings.
  • To check it loaded: type PBHELLO. If AutoCAD says Unknown command, the zip was not fully extracted before install.cmd ran.

First run#

The plugin looks for your parts library by itself when AutoCAD starts. It prints one line, such as Found your library: drawings in …, models and photos in …, data files in … (6,412 of 6,516 parts). The search runs in the background and is saved, so later sessions skip it.

If nothing is found, it asks you to run PBSETUP, which opens the setup window.

  1. To choose the library yourself, browse to the folder that holds your part drawings and data files. The window shows what it found. No part drawings found means you picked the wrong folder.
  2. Paste a pricing token if you have one and press Test connection. You can leave it blank.

Without a library, parts place as labeled placeholder blocks.

Your first layout#

  1. Type PBCATALOG.
  2. Type square deck and double-click the first row. The deck takes height 48.
  3. Click at 0,0, then at 48,0, then press Enter.

You now have two decks side by side with their posts. Each part brings its own posts. Where two parts share a post there is one post, sized for the taller part.

Each post has a dome cap on top. Caps follow their post when it is added, re-sized, moved or removed. Posts under a roof, canopy or topper get no cap. A flat cap or medallion you place on a post replaces its dome. The parts list counts one dome cap per capped post.

  1. Type PBPANEL. The panel counts and prices the decks and posts, and updates after every edit.

One UNDO takes back a part and its posts together.

Place parts#

PBSTAMP#

PBSTAMP keeps placing the part you chose. The part follows the cursor and snaps to matching connection points. The words at the cursor say what it is snapped to, or why it cannot attach and where it could. Click inside a placed deck to stack the next storey on it. Click to place, and press Enter to finish.

While placing, type:

Type What it does
Height Changes the deck height for the next clicks.
Next Attaches by the other side. The words say which side, such as “2 of 2”.
Angle Asks for an angle, such as 45 or -30, for parts that do not snap.
Rotate / Counter Turns the part one step (30° unless you change it) one way or the other.
Turn Turns the part 180°.
Free Turns the snapping off, and on again.

The angle stays for the rest of the run, so a row of benches all face the same way. A part that snaps takes its angle from what it attaches to.

Over a post, a panel swings around that post with the cursor. Aim it and click, and its far post is placed with it. Where nothing else lines it up, it turns in steps.

Drag from the catalog#

Drag a part from the catalog into the drawing. It lands where you drop it and snaps like a click. It asks nothing:

  • a deck takes the height the catalog offers;
  • a slide takes the first sit-down wall or hood on the list;
  • a roof on a structure with no age range takes its lowest height.

One line says what it took. Change a height with PBHEIGHT.

A deck double-clicked in the catalog places at once at the offered height. The command line shows the height; type Height while placing to change it. Typed, PBSTAMP asks for the height first.

The turn step#

The step starts at 30°. After that, it stays at whatever you set. To change it, pick a value in the TURN STEP box on the catalog: 5, 10, 15, 22.5, 30, 45 or 90. It sets Rotate, Counter, the ⟲ ⟳ buttons, PBROT15 and the swing on a post.

The step is AutoCAD’s polar angle, so the box and AutoCAD’s Drafting Settings change the same value. For any other angle, type POLARANG and the angle.

Turn parts already placed#

Select them and type PBROT15 (counter-clockwise) or PBROTBACK15 (clockwise). Each turns one step. The ⟲ ⟳ buttons on the catalog do the same. A selection that includes a deck turns as one structure around its first deck.

To line a part up with a fence, use AutoCAD’s ROTATE with the Reference option: pick two points along the part, type Points, then pick two points along the fence.

Place by hand#

Plain AutoCAD works too. A part you move into place by hand gets the same posts, fillers and price as one that snapped.

  • Every connection point, posts included, is a Node snap target. MOVE a part by its connection point onto its neighbour’s.
  • After a MOVE, COPY, ROTATE, STRETCH or grip edit, the command line says what followed, such as Posts follow the MOVE: 2 added, 0 re-sized, 4 removed.
  • A part left a little short is not attached. The command line says so, such as S-1101-R5 at (0, 0) is 2" from S-1101-R5 at (43, 0): not attached. MOVE it again with Node snap. PBAUDIT lists every such pair.
  • A block put in with AutoCAD’s INSERT counts after its next edit.
  • One UNDO takes back a move and everything that followed it.

Roofs#

Roofs, shade canopies and toppers go on last.

  1. Set the structure’s age range first (the settings row, or PBSTRUCTURE).
  2. PBSTAMP the roof and move the cursor over the deck. The roof snaps where it should stand, centred on the deck. A roof or canopy that covers several decks snaps to where the cursor is over them. Toppers still snap to an edge or a post.
  3. The command line says the height it took, such as Roof over 36" deck → 105" (69" clearance, ages 2-5).
  • The height is the highest deck under the roof’s posts, rounded up to whole feet, plus the roof’s clearance for the age range. The roof’s posts are sized for it.
  • A roof takes no deck edge. Parts already on the edges do not stop it.
  • With no age range set, or the snapping off, the command asks for the deck height under the roof. Type a height, or Ground for a roof with no deck.
  • PBHEIGHT on a deck moves the roof over it, and keeps a raise.
  • To raise a roof, type PBHEIGHT, then Roof, click the roof and type the height. Roofs go up in 12" steps from their lowest height.
  • The roof’s height label shows on plan. Editing it by hand is checked: the command line says when a roof is below its lowest height, and PBAUDIT lists it.
  • PBSETTINGS roofoverride=1 adds 1 ft (0 to 3) to every roof and shade canopy placed after it. The design file’s Roof Height Override is read on import and written on export.

Set up each structure#

The settings row at the top of PBCATALOG says what you are building next.

Setting Choices
Age 2-5, 5-12, 2-12, 6-23, 9-14, 13+ or 14+
System R5, R35, RH5, RH3 or RV5
Mounting In ground or Surface mount
Posts Steel or Aluminum
Deck A deck height. PBSTAMP offers it first when you place a deck.
Show Structure parts, Independent items (swings, freestanding, fitness and site items) or All
IPEMA only Tick box
Canada Tick box

Each click filters the catalog and the command line says what was set, such as Drawing default set: ages 2-5, R5. One UNDO takes back one click. If a command is still running, the click waits: press Enter or Esc and click again.

What the row does#

  • The catalog hides parts that do not fit. The line under the row says how many, such as 38 parts hidden by the drawing's settings (ages 2-5, R5). Tick Show hidden parts to see them, each with its reason. You can still place a hidden part. The command line tells you it is hidden and why.
  • Parts you place take the row’s settings. Each part keeps the settings it was placed with. Changing the row does not change parts already placed, or their posts.
  • Deck heights are checked for the age. PBSTAMP and PBHEIGHT refuse a height the deck is not rated for, such as S-1101-R5 is not rated for 84" in a 2-5 structure (its 2-5 band rates 6"-72").
  • Posts follow mounting and material. Surface mount and aluminum change the post part numbers, such as S-1008-R35-08ft-SMA.
  • A deck’s posts match the deck. An R5 deck gets R5 posts and an R35 deck gets R35 posts, whatever the row says.

Build the next structure#

Change the row and keep placing. The parts you already placed keep their settings.

Change parts already placed#

  1. Select the parts.
  2. Click Apply the row to selection under the row.

The parts take the row’s settings and their posts are re-picked. Parts that do not fit are listed with the reason and selected. One UNDO takes it back.

When you select parts placed with different settings from the row, a line under the row says so, such as 12 selected parts were placed with ages 2-5, R35.

  • Use these puts those settings in the row.
  • Apply the row to them gives the parts the row’s settings.

Copies#

COPY, MIRROR and ARRAY copies keep the settings of the parts they were copied from.

Canada#

Tick Canada in the row. The catalog hides parts made only for the US, and placed parts made only for the US are listed. Canada is one setting for the whole drawing.

Clear all#

Clear all sets every setting in the row back to not set, turns Canada off, sets Show to All, and clears the search, family, price, fit filter and sort. Placed parts keep their settings.

Imported designs and named structures#

  • Importing a .kcd fills the row from the tab you import.
  • PBSTRUCTURE gives a named group settings of its own. Click any part of the group and answer the prompts. Press Enter to keep a value.
  • When you select a group with its own settings, a line under the row shows them, with Use these and Save the row to the group.

From the keyboard#

PBSETTINGS does what a click does. Type one setting at a time:

Type What it does
age=2-5, system=R5, mounting=surface, material=aluminum, ipema=yes, region=Canada, deck=48, show=structure Sets one setting in the row. An empty value, such as age=, sets it back to not set.
apply Gives the selected parts the row’s settings.
use Puts the selected parts’ settings in the row.
use=TOWER Puts the TOWER group’s settings in the row.
saveto=TOWER Gives the TOWER group the row’s settings.
clear Sets the whole row back to not set.

Name each structure for the quote#

Group each play structure and give it a name. The parts list and the quote have one section per named group.

Parts in no group are listed by family, such as Decks, Posts and Freestanding Slides. Site items such as benches and trash cans go under Site Amenities. Swings, freestanding items and site amenities can stay loose. Group a structure to list it under its own name.

To name a structure:

  1. Type CLASSICGROUP.
  2. Type the name under Group Name and click New.
  3. Select the structure’s parts and press Enter.
  4. Click OK.

The name you type is the heading on the quote, such as 5-12 Composite. AutoCAD does not allow / in a name, so write 5-12, not 5/12.

  • Posts, fillers and parts that snap onto a grouped part join its group.
  • A swing set saved as its own drawing and brought in with XREF gets its own section.
  • Do not make a structure into a block. Parts inside a block are left out of the parts list and the quote.

Start from a design you already have#

  • A .kcd design file: PBKCDINFO shows what is in each tab without changing the drawing. PBIMPORTKCD brings one tab in at its exact positions and heights. Importing the same tab again replaces it.
  • An older flat Playcraft drawing: PBMIGRATEZ lifts parts to their real heights. Parts it cannot place for certain stay where they are and are listed. You can run it more than once.

PBEXPORTKCD writes the drawing back out to a .kcd. PBDEPS checks a .kcd for missing attachments and broken links.

An imported design is fully built, so most connection points are already taken. If a part will not snap, run PBMATES: grey points are taken.

Find a part in the catalog#

PBCATALOG opens with the cursor in the search box. The best matches come first.

Type Finds
square deck Parts with both words, in any order.
"sit down" Parts with that exact phrase.
slide -castle Slides, leaving out parts with “castle”.
S-1101-R5, s1101r5 That part. Dashes, spaces and case do not matter.
1101 That part in every rail system.
castel Close misspellings still match. The panel shows what it searched for.

The family and price filters and the settings row work together with the search. Click a column header to sort, and click it again to reverse. Parts with no price show a dash and stay at the bottom.

Storeys and the rulebook#

PBLEVEL#

PBLEVEL lists which storey each part stands on. Type a level number to select everything on it. Answer Layers to put each storey on its own layer (PB_LVL0, PB_LVL1, and so on). Freeze a layer to hide that storey while you work on the one above. Frozen storeys are still counted and exported. Delete the PB_LVL layers to stop.

PBAUDIT#

PBAUDIT checks the drawing against the design team’s rules and lists what breaks them:

  • decks 108" and up with an open edge;
  • stepping distances, Geo-Wave and traverse heights;
  • ground panels under a deck too tall for them;
  • the wrong limb climber for the deck;
  • mirrored or scaled parts;
  • parts that almost touch;
  • parts that do not fit the settings they were placed with.

Each finding names the part and the wiki card the rule comes from. Each rule has a number that never changes, such as AUD-01, then what it asks for and who acts:

  • Fix: change the drawing;
  • Decide: it may be right as drawn, you decide;
  • Needs information: the drawing does not say enough to check it.

PBAUDIT only reports. It never changes the drawing.

Make the paperwork#

  1. Save the drawing.
  2. Type PBPAPER, or click Make Paperwork on the BOM panel (PBPANEL).

There are no prompts. A folder named <drawing> paperwork appears beside the drawing with the checks, the 3D model, the sheets PDF and the parts workbook. The command line ends with a list of what was made and what is missing.

The pictures render in Blender in the background. When the command line says they are ready, run PBPAPER again to place them on the sheets. With no Blender the sheets show empty picture frames.

PBPAPERDRAFT does the same with quicker, rougher pictures.

Plain AutoCAD#

MOVE, COPY, ERASE, ROTATE, layers, groups, xrefs, Properties and UNDO all work on Playcraft parts. Posts follow MOVE, COPY, ERASE and grips. PBGEN checks them. PBFILL brings the fillers up to date.

Watch out for these:

Command What happens
EXPLODE The part becomes plain lines and leaves the quote. UNDO brings it back. Change its layer or colour instead.
MOVE with an object snap The part takes the height of the point you snapped to. The command line says so. Use PBHEIGHT to change heights. OSNAPZ set to 1 stops snaps changing heights.
COPY of an imported part The copy shares the original’s identity until you run PBEXPORTKCD, which gives each copy its own.
MIRROR, SCALE The part number does not change, so it is priced as the standard part. Handed parts have their own part numbers in the catalog.

Commands also list what they skipped, such as parts on frozen layers or inside blocks. These lines are not errors.

New in this build#

  • Paperwork in one step. PBPAPER, or Make Paperwork on the BOM panel, makes the checks, model, sheets PDF and workbook for a saved drawing. See Make the paperwork above.

  • Perspective views. PBSHEETS now ends the set with L-901 Perspective views: the hero render large, with the alternate, aerial and plan renders beside it, each with a short caption. It uses the pictures in renders\<drawing name>\ beside the drawing; it does not make them. A view not rendered yet shows a frame saying where to put it.

  • Cover and project data. PBSHEETS now starts the set with G-001 Cover and G-002 Project data. The cover has the project, the issue and revision, the sheet index, who is who by role, the key figures and a note to verify the site. It places the hero render from renders\<drawing name>\<drawing name>-hero.png beside the drawing: export the model with PBEXPORTGLB, render it, then run PBSHEETS again. With no render the cover says where to put it. G-002 lists each structure (system, ages, size, highest deck, fall height, child capacity, parts), the parts by family, the accessibility counts provided and required, and the site requirements. Figures the drawing cannot give print [needs designer input].

  • Sheet numbers. The sheets are numbered the US National CAD Standard way: a discipline letter, a sheet type and a number. The set is G-001 Cover, G-002 Project data, L-001 General notes, L-101 Site plan, L-102 Footing plan, L-103 Component plan, L-201 Elevations, L-601 Equipment schedule, L-602 Colour and finish and L-901 Perspective views, in that order. A sheet that runs on takes a letter: L-601A, L-601B.

  • Elevations. PBSHEETS also makes L-201 Elevations, one sheet per structure (L-201, L-201A …): a front view from the south over a side view from the east, drawn from the parts’ 3D models with hidden lines removed, each on a finished grade line, at the largest standard scale that fits. Each view shows the highest deck and the overall height from grade. Parts with no 3D model are named on the sheet, not drawn.

  • Site plan. PBSHEETS also makes L-101 Site plan: the whole site at the largest standard scale that fits, each structure’s use zone dashed and named, slide exit zones hatched orange, and the areas in sq ft beside it. The use zones come from the parts. Zones that overlap, or run past the play area, are listed on the sheet and in the command line. Your own photo, dimensions and text show on the plan as you drew them.

  • Play area. Draw the play area as a closed polyline (any shape) on a layer named Play area. The site plan uses it as the surfacing area. A closed ring of border timbers works too. With neither, the sheet shows the use zones as the minimum surfacing area.

  • Sheets. PBSHEETS makes the layout L-103 Component plan on ANSI B (17 x 11): the parts’ line work at the largest standard scale that fits, with the title block filled in from the design file. Each part has a numbered bubble, listed in the call-out key beside the plan. A long key continues on L-103A. Deck heights print upright in boxes. Blank fields print a line to fill in by hand. Run it again after changes; it only refreshes its own layouts. PBSHEETPDF plots the sheets to one PDF, in colour. The sheet shows the colour fills when they are on in the drawing, and leaves them out when they are off.

  • Equipment schedule. PBSHEETS also makes L-601 Equipment schedule: one table per structure with the call-out number, part, description, family, quantity and colours. The numbers match the bubbles on L-103 and the quantities match PBBOM. No prices. Each structure starts a new sheet, and a long table continues on the next (L-601A, L-601B). PBSHEETPDF puts every sheet in one PDF, one page per sheet, in sheet order.

  • Footing plan. PBSHEETS also makes L-102 Footing plan, after the general notes: every hole drawn true size over a faint plan, four setting-out points A to D round them, and a hole table. Holes are numbered in digging order. Each hole has two tapes, each from a named point; the hole centre is where they meet. MARK in the table is the footing’s mark used on the rest of the set. Beside the plan: the footing types by hole size, the setting-out sides and diagonals, and the posts by length. Overlapping holes carry a star. A post with four or more footings close round it is ringed (E1, E2) and drawn larger on the next sheet, with each hole measured from the post. Where the data gives no size, the sheet uses the standard 12 x 12 x 18 and a note names the type. A part that stands on the ground with no footing data is listed under the types as details to follow. A long hole table continues on L-102A.

  • Colour and finish. PBSHEETS also makes L-602 Colour and finish: each colour setting with a swatch, the colour, its RAL number, the material and the call-out numbers of the parts that take it, the same rows as the workbook’s Colours tab. Beside it: the colours used, the finish, and any part coloured apart from its structure. A long table continues on L-602A.

  • General notes. PBSHEETS also makes L-001 General notes, first in the set: numbered notes on field checks, use zones, surfacing by others, installation and the accessible route, and the standards cited. Long notes continue on L-001A.

  • Sheet fonts. The plugin installs the sheet fonts (Outfit and IBM Plex Sans) for you the first time it loads. Restart AutoCAD once after that, then run PBSHEETS again. Until then the sheet uses Arial.

  • Colour fills. Tick Colour fills on the catalog to fill each part in plan with a soft shade of its colours, under its lines. Clear it for a black-and-white print. PBCOLORFILL does the same. The fills are on the PB_Fill layer and move, copy and erase with their parts. Roofs get no fill. The fills change colour with every colour change, and U takes them back with the colour.

  • Project line. The top of the catalog panel shows the number of structures and the total MSRP (the same total as PBPRICE). When some lines have no price, or PBAUDIT finds something, it shows the count. Click the count to run the command.

  • Structure on the card. The selection card says which structure the part is in. Select structure selects all of it (PBSELECT Structure). A part in no group gets Select connected instead.

  • Shorter card for many parts. With several kinds selected, decks come first and posts, caps and fillers share one line (“14 posts · 8 caps”). Click the line to list them.

  • Top ten fits. Fits here lists the ten parts used most. Click Show all for the rest.

  • Select by kind. The catalog’s Select list, beside the search box, selects all decks, roofs and canopies, ground events, elevated events, posts, or everything connected to the selected part. It runs PBSELECT, which you can also type. It leaves an ordinary AutoCAD selection, so MOVE, ERASE and Properties work on it. Parts inside a block or on a locked or frozen layer are left out, and the command line says how many.

  • Next step. Select one deck and a short list shows under the selection card: access, points used, the free point, slides with no wall, a roof, and posts you set. ✓ is done, → is the next step, ○ is open. Click a link to show the right tab or select the part. PBNEXT prints the same list. These are hints for configuring, not a check against any standard.

  • Height up or down one step. Select decks or a roof and run PBHEIGHTUP or PBHEIGHTDOWN. A deck moves 6", a roof 12". A step past the part’s rating is refused and nothing moves. Roofs, posts and fillers follow. In the selection card, − and + either side of the Height list run them. At the rated limit the button is off; hover it to see why.

  • Post tools that stay. Select posts, or a deck for all its posts. PBPOSTUP and PBPOSTDOWN make them a foot longer or shorter. PBPOSTCUT cuts them to their deck, with a flat cap. PBPOSTCAP swaps the dome cap for a flat one and back. PBGEN keeps what you set and says so. PBPOSTRULE puts the posts back on the rule. Select posts and the selection card has a button for each.

  • Walls follow their slide. MOVE or ROTATE a slide or climber and its wall goes with it. PBAUDIT names a slide or climber with no wall.

  • No silent clicks. A panel click while a command runs, a part placed on a layer that is off or locked, or a wall that cannot follow its slide now says so on the command line, with what to do.

  • Most used, at heights with few designs. When few designs had the deck at its height, the list also counts the heights 6" either side, at a lower weight. The Used column still shows the designs at this height; ~9 means only nearby heights had it. Hover a count to see both.

  • New catalog layout. The drawing settings show as chips under the search box. Click the gear or a chip to change them. The list has three tabs: Fits here, Over it and All parts, each with its count. It works at 440 pixels wide. Dock it wider to see the System and Family columns. Hover a part to see its photo, or its plan symbol when it has no photo.

  • Fits here. Select a placed part. The tab lists only the parts that fit one of its free mate points and are rated for its height and age band. The line above the list says what is hidden and why. Show hidden lists those parts, dimmed. Double-click a part to place it on that point. Pick a point in the list beside that line to use only that one. Click All parts to see everything again.

  • The selected part. A card shows its name and part number, its height and rated range, free points, and posts and caps. Details shows the rest. Pick a height to run PBHEIGHT on the selected decks. Rebuild posts runs PBGEN, Fillers runs PBFILL, and the turn buttons turn it. Colours opens its colours. U takes each one back. The part stays selected after each of these.

  • Over it. Select a deck. The tab lists the roofs and canopies that go over it, each with the height it would take. Double-click one to place it, centred over the deck. If the deck already has a roof, it says which one.

  • Most used first. On a selected deck, Fits here and Over it list first the parts finished designs used most on that deck at that height. The Used column shows how many designs. Click a column header to sort another way.

  • Goes with. Under the card, up to 3 parts that are usually in the same design, for example “In 92% of designs with this slide: …”. Show lists that part in the catalog. It does not place it.

  • The parts list is an Excel workbook. PBEXPORTBOM saves an .xlsx: a Summary tab, one tab per structure and a Colours tab. Name the file .csv to get the old CSV.

  • The 3D view is in colour. PB3D shows each part from its 3D drawing, in the drawing’s colour scheme. A part with no 3D drawing is shown from its older 3D model. A part shows as an orange box until its model is ready. The banner lists the parts shown from older models.

  • Save the design as a 3D model. PBEXPORTGLB saves the drawing as one .glb file, real size and in the 3D view’s colours. Windows’ 3D Viewer, phones and Blender open it. Each structure is a group with its name, and each part is named by its part number. A part with no 3D model is an orange box, and the command line names it.

  • Pick the colours. Choose a scheme in the Colours list on the catalog. PBSCHEME does the same: type it, then the scheme’s name (? lists them). The drawing keeps the scheme when saved.

  • Colour each structure. Select a structure. The catalog shows a COLOURS section with its scheme and its colours by heading (Posts, Decks, Slides and so on). Pick a colour on a heading to change every colour under it, or open the heading to change one. Apply to all structures gives every structure the same colours. To colour one part only, turn group selection off (Ctrl+Shift+A), select the part and pick its colour. Clear part colours and Clear structure changes take colours off. PBCOLOR does the same by typing: ? lists the colours, then for example Posts=Blue, scheme=Earth, all or clear=parts. One U undoes each change. A copied structure keeps its colours.

  • Colour all of one material. Select a structure, type PBCOLOR, then for example family=powdercoat colour=Blue. Each powder coat colour of that structure that comes in Blue turns Blue. A panel face or core changes only if the panel comes in that colour with its other side as it is. The command line names anything it skips. The other families are playtuff, roto, hdpe_solid, hdpe_face, hdpe_core, shade_std and shade_prem. The Colour a family row at the top of COLOURS does the same.

  • Colours show their RAL. The COLOURS lists and PBCOLOR name each colour by its product name and RAL number, for example “Pacific Blue · RAL 5005”, with a swatch in the real colour. You still type the short name: Posts=Blue.

  • The company can set its own colours. If your company keeps a colour file in a shared folder, run PBSETUP and put that folder under Company colours. Without one, the built-in colours are used. The 3D view says which colours it uses, at its top left, under the hint. When the colour file is saved again, type PBCOLORTABLE: the 3D view and the fills show the new colours. If the file cannot be read, the colours in use are kept.

  • The BOM and the quote say the colours. PBBOM, PBPRICE and PBEXPORTBOM show each part’s colours under its line, then a colour schedule for each structure: each colour and how many parts take it.

  • Colours go to and from the design file. PBIMPORTKCD brings in each tab’s scheme, its changed colours and the parts’ own colours. PBEXPORTKCD writes them back.

  • Posts come with their caps. Every post gets its dome cap on top, in plan and in 3D. See Your first layout.

  • Drag parts into the drawing. Drag a part from the catalog and drop it. See Drag from the catalog.

  • A double-clicked deck places at once. It takes the offered height. Type Height while placing to change it.

  • Roofs are dashed, and a Roofs box hides them. Roofs, shade canopies and toppers show as a dashed outline of their real size, with a small level height label below the middle, so the deck under them reads. Tick or clear Roofs on the catalog to show or hide them. PBROOFS does the same.

  • A roof that does not fit its deck is refused. A roof whose posts would stand inside the deck, such as a hex roof on square decks or an R3.5 roof on an R5 deck, is not placed. The command line says why. PBFIT on the deck lists the parts made for it. Free places it anyway.

  • Roofs over a deck with no age range set. The roof stands over the deck at its lowest clearance instead of asking.

  • Set the turn step. The TURN STEP box on the catalog sets how far parts turn at a time. It starts at 30°. See The turn step.

  • A Post heights box. Tick or clear Post heights on the catalog to show or hide each post’s height ring. PBPOSTHEIGHTS does the same.

  • Roofs snap over the deck. Move a roof or shade canopy anywhere over its deck and it snaps there. You no longer have to aim at the deck’s edge. See Roofs.

  • The plugin finds your library by itself. It finds your parts library even when drawings, models and data files are in different folders. PBSETUP shows what it found.

  • Posts show their height. Each post has a ring with its height in it on the PostFootings layer. Turn the layer on or off to show or hide it.

  • The panel lists each structure on its own. PBPANEL shows a heading row per structure with its MSRP and REP subtotal, then its parts.

  • Roofs take their height from the deck and the age range. Snap a roof to a deck edge and its height and posts are worked out. See Roofs.

  • Loose parts are listed by family. Decks, posts and fillers in no group go under their family, such as Decks and Posts, not Unassigned.

  • Plain messages. What the plugin prints on the command line and in the panel is short and plain, like this guide.

  • Parts keep the settings they were placed with. Change the row to start the next structure. Parts already placed, and their posts, do not change.

  • A deck’s posts match the deck’s own system.

  • Apply the row to selection gives selected parts the row’s settings.

  • Copies keep their settings.

  • BOM descriptions come from the pricing database. Imported designs no longer show an ID instead of a name, and the same part shares one line.

  • Imported designs keep their numbers. Tested against 819 website designs:

    • Aluminum posts keep their price after PBGEN.
    • PBFILL follows the design’s automatic-fillers setting and leaves hand-placed fillers alone.
    • PBGEN keeps post lengths the design already got right.
    • Collar counts match the design file on far more designs.
    • PBAUDIT no longer flags traverses, stepping distances, towers and limb climbers that are fine.

See Set up each structure.

Every command#

* means the command needs the component library.

Setup#

Command What it does
PBSETUP Opens the setup window for the library folder and pricing token.
PBVAULT Sets the library folders from the command line. Find searches this computer for them.

Place parts#

Command What it does
PBCATALOG * Opens the catalog. Search, filter, sort, and double-click a part to place it.
PBFIT * Select or click a placed part. The catalog then lists only parts that fit its free points.
PBSTAMP * Places parts one after another, snapped to the parts already there.
PBDROP * What a drag from the catalog runs. Places one part with no questions.
PBROT15, PBROTBACK15 Turns the selected parts one step (30° unless changed) counter-clockwise or clockwise.
PBATTACH * Click near a deck edge to attach the 24" Poly Wave Slide.
PBMATES Shows every connection point: green is free, grey is taken. Run it again to hide them.

Structure#

Command What it does
PBGEN Checks every post and puts it right.
PBPOSTHEIGHTS Shows or hides the ring with each post’s height. Answer On or Off.
PBROOFS Shows or hides roofs, shade canopies and toppers. Answer On or Off.
PBCOLORFILL Shows or hides the soft colour fills under the parts. Answer On or Off.
PBFILL * Places barrier fillers where decks of different heights share posts.
PBHEIGHT Changes a deck’s height. Click a deck, or press Enter for every deck.
PBSETTINGS Sets the catalog’s settings row from the keyboard.
PBSTRUCTURE Gives a named group settings of its own.
PBREGION Sets the drawing to US or Canada.
PBLEVEL Lists storeys, selects one, or puts each on its own layer.
PBMIGRATEZ Lifts an older flat drawing to real heights.

3D#

Command What it does
PB3D * Opens a live 3D view. Drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Click a part to select it.
PBCOLORTABLE Reads the company colour file again and says which colours are in use.

Count and price#

Command What it does
PBBOM Prints the parts list, one section per structure. Works offline.
PBPRICE Prints the parts list with MSRP and REP prices.
PBEXPORTBOM Saves the priced parts list as an Excel workbook, or as a CSV if the name ends .csv.
PBPANEL Opens a live parts list with prices, one section per structure.
PBSYNC Refreshes the catalog data saved in the drawing. Needs the pricing token.

Sheets#

Command What it does
PBSHEETS Makes the sheet layouts with the title block filled in. Run it again to refresh them.
PBSHEETPDF Plots the sheets to one PDF.
PBPAPER Makes the checks, model, sheets PDF and workbook in one folder beside the saved drawing.
PBPAPERDRAFT The same as PBPAPER with draft pictures.

Design files (.kcd)#

Command What it does
PBKCDINFO Shows the part, post and filler counts in each tab of a .kcd.
PBIMPORTKCD * Imports one tab of a .kcd.
PBEXPORTKCD * Writes the drawing to a .kcd, plus a .json copy.
PBDEPS * Checks a .kcd for missing attachments and broken links.

Checks#

Command What it does
PBAUDIT Checks the drawing against the design team’s rules.
PBHELLO Prints the build you have loaded.
PBNUDGE Used by test scripts. Use MOVE instead.
PBLIBCHECK Checks the part library. For library maintainers.

If something looks wrong#

Problem What to do
Parts place as placeholder blocks The library is not found. Run PBSETUP, or type PBVAULT then Find to search again.
A part will not snap Run PBMATES. Grey means the point is taken. If it is green and still will not snap, report it.
A part is missing from the catalog The settings row hides it. Tick Show hidden parts.
A search finds nothing Check the settings row and family filter, or click Clear all. A leading - leaves a word out, so remove it from a pasted part number.
A part is missing from the quote It was exploded. UNDO brings it back.
PBBOM lists a structure’s decks under Decks, not its name Its parts are in no named group. See Name each structure for the quote.
One structure shows as two sections Its parts are in two groups. Put them in one.
A whole structure is missing from the parts list It was made into a block. Explode the block and group the parts.
Two parts look joined but get extra posts They are a little apart. MOVE one with Node snap.
Setup says No part drawings found Pick the folder that holds your part drawings and data files.
PB commands are missing Check you have AutoCAD 2027 and that the zip was fully extracted before install.cmd ran.
No live prices Run PBSETUP, paste the token and press Test connection. Built-in prices still work.

Tell us what you find#

Send four lines:

Build:      (the line PBHELLO prints)
Doing:      what you did, in order
Expected:   what you thought would happen
Happened:   what actually happened

The Expected line tells us whether it is a bug or a different idea of how it should work.

Things worth telling us:

  • where a part would not snap and the words at the cursor did not say why;
  • a count, price or post height that is wrong;
  • a part you searched for that was not in the first few rows (tell us what you typed);
  • a PBAUDIT finding you would have signed off, or a problem it missed.

A screenshot of the command line helps. The drawing helps most.
